1. Scope

The contract between debounce and its two neighbours: the pin that drives din and the logic that reads dout. The block has no software interface. It turns a raw, asynchronous contact (button, switch) into a signal that is synchronous to clk and changes only after the contact has rested.

2.2. Generics and derived numbers

Everything below is counted in clocks and follows from one constant of the design: 

C_LIMIT = G_CLK_HZ / 1000 * G_DEBOUNCE_MS     (integer arithmetic, divided first)
Name Value Meaning
SAMPLES C_LIMIT + 1 equal samples of din needed before dout changes
LATENCY C_LIMIT + 3 clock edges from a change of din to that of dout
INTERVAL C_LIMIT + 1 least number of clocks between two changes of dout

With the defaults (100 MHz, 10 ms) C_LIMIT is 1 000 000, so the contact must rest for 1 000 001 clocks: 10 ns longer than the 10 ms asked for.

Two limits of the arithmetic:

  • G_CLK_HZ below 1000 makes C_LIMIT zero, which the design rejects when it is elaborated (range error on a positive constant).
  • G_CLK_HZ is cut to whole kHz, so 32 768 Hz counts as 32 000 Hz and the time comes out 2.3 % short.

3. Register map

None. The block has no bus and no registers that software can reach.

4. Timing contracts

din is sampled at every rising edge of clk. A sample is the level of din at that edge; what it does between two edges is not seen.

4.1. Filter

dout takes a level only after din showed that level at SAMPLES consecutive clock edges. One sample of the other level starts the count again.

In time, for a pulse on din of width W and a clock period T:

W Result
W <= C_LIMIT x T never passed on
W >= (C_LIMIT + 1) x T always passed on
between the two depends on where the clock edges fall

An input that never rests for SAMPLES clocks is never passed on, however long that goes on. That is the purpose of the block, not a fault.

Enforced by
p_tw_filter

4.2. Latency

When din changes and then rests, dout follows at the LATENCY-th rising edge after the change: the first two edges move the level through the synchronizer, the next SAMPLES count it. In time that is more than (C_LIMIT + 2) and at most (C_LIMIT + 3) clock periods.

Enforced by
p_tw_latency

4.3. Minimum interval

Two changes of dout are at least INTERVAL clocks apart, so a counter or an edge detector behind it never sees a pulse shorter than that. A change of din taken back as soon as it was passed on is ignored like any other short pulse.

Enforced by
p_tw_min_interval

4.4. Reset

dout is low from the clock after rst is sampled high and stays low while rst is high, whatever din does. The count starts again when rst is released.

A contact that is closed during reset therefore reads as open first: dout rises SAMPLES clocks after rst is released. Logic that acts on a rising dout sees an edge that the contact did not make.

Enforced by
p_tw_reset

4.5. What the neighbours may do

There is no illegal input: din may do anything at any time. The table lists the behaviour that looks like a fault from outside and is not.

The input does The block does Checked by
Bounces, each rest shorter than SAMPLES Nothing, then one change when it rests TC-10
Glitches at any phase of the clock Nothing TC-11
Toggles without ever resting Nothing, for as long as it lasts TC-12
Changes back as dout changes Keeps the new level TC-14
rst while a count is running Starts the count again TC-13

5. Error and recovery

The block reports no error and cannot lock up: every state is left by rst or by a resting input. It keeps no state that a software restart could leave stale.

A sample taken while din is changing can settle either way. The two synchronizer flip-flops give it a clock to settle; the filter then treats it as one more bounce. Metastability is not simulated and the settling time was not analysed for a device.
